About

I am an Assistant Professor in the Department of Electrical Engineering and Computer Science at the University of Wyoming, since Fall 2025. Before joining Wyoming, I was a Research Assistant Professor in Computer Science and Engineering at the New York University Tandon School of Engineering, and an Assistant Professor at the Center for Visual Information Technology, IIIT-Hyderabad.

I received my Ph.D. in Computer Science and Engineering from Michigan State University, advised by Dr. Arun Ross, with a thesis committee of Dr. Anil K. Jain, Dr. Selin Aviyente and Dr. Yiying Tong. My research spans face morph detection, facial age editing with diffusion models, template protection, camera sensor identification from iris images, near-duplicate image analysis, and biometrics for extended reality.

Research

Recent projects

Three current lines of work: verifying people inside VR headsets, restoring age and identity cues hidden by makeup, and testing whether multimodal language models can read fingerprints.

CVIP 2026 · acceptedNSF REU 2026

Ocular verification for virtual reality

Headsets such as Meta Quest and Apple Vision Pro authenticate users with eye images captured at close range, off-axis and under uneven light. We test how well ISO/IEC 29794-6 iris quality metrics hold up on VR-captured eyes, use generative models to correct off-axis gaze, illumination and specular reflections, and compare iris, periocular and fused recognition.

~11%lower EER with iris + periocular fusion
3parts: quality, correction, matching

H. Singh, R. Tran, A. Kharel, S. Banerjee

DiffClean: removing makeup to recover age and identity

Makeup can make a minor look older and can change perceived identity, which weakens age checks meant to protect young users online. DiffClean is a reference-free, text-guided diffusion model that erases makeup from a face image while keeping the person's identity and age cues, so age estimation and face verification work on the cleaned image.

4losses guide the diffusion model
2gains: age estimation and verification

E. Gavas, S. Banerjee, C. Hegde, N. Memon

CVPR 2026 Workshop · FoundGen-Bio

FPBench: can multimodal LLMs read fingerprints?

Multimodal large language models already describe faces and irises, but fingerprints carry fine ridge structure that is hard to put into words. FPBench is the first benchmark of its kind: it tests open-source and proprietary models on biometric and forensic fingerprint tasks with zero-shot and chain-of-thought prompts, and reports where they succeed, how they explain their answers, and where they fail.

20MLLMs evaluated
7real and synthetic datasets
8biometric and forensic tasks

E. Gavas, S. Banerjee, C. Hegde, N. Memon

From Ph.D. and NYU work
Face morphs

Detecting morphs and recovering the hidden identity

Conditional identity disentanglement detects morphs against a trusted reference and recovers the accomplice's face; de-morphing extracts both component faces from a single morph.

IJCB 2021 · IJCB 2022
Aging & editing

Identity-preserving face aging with diffusion

Latent diffusion models age and de-age faces from few examples while keeping identity, and attribute edits are mitigated so face recognition still works.

IJCB 2023 · T-BIOM 2024
Sensor forensics

Camera sensor identification from iris images

PRNU-based sensor identification on near-infrared iris images, the effect of photometric preprocessing, and sensor de-identification that preserves recognition.

ISBA 2019 · Best Paper
Image phylogeny

How near-duplicate images evolved

Basis-function models estimate transformations between near-duplicate face and iris images and build a phylogeny tree from the original to its descendants.

BTAS 2019 · Best Paper · T-BIOM 2025
